Remote evaluation of Quantum Random Number Generators, from every vendor, in one place.

About QRNG Zoo

QRNG Zoo is a research platform and cloud service, built by the Computer Architecture and Security Lab (CASLAB) at Northwestern University, for the evaluation of Quantum Random Number Generator (QRNG) PCIe cards. The platform provides remote access to QRNG devices from different vendors, allowing researchers, developers, and vendors themselves to benchmark and evaluate the quality of the randomness each device produces.

Beyond evaluating individual devices, QRNG Zoo also explores mixing entropy from multiple, independent vendors. Combining randomness sources sourced from different hardware designs and manufacturers is a way to maximize security: even if one vendor's device were ever compromised, biased, or found to have a subtle design flaw, entropy mixed in from the other independent sources helps ensure the combined output remains unpredictable.

Why a QRNG Zoo?

True random numbers are a foundational building block for cryptography, secure protocols, and many scientific applications. Quantum Random Number Generators promise randomness rooted in the fundamentally probabilistic nature of quantum mechanics, rather than in deterministic algorithms. But not all QRNG hardware is created equal, and independent, apples-to-apples evaluation of QRNG PCIe cards from different vendors is difficult for any single lab or company to do on its own.

  • Remote access to a shared rack of QRNG PCIe cards from multiple vendors, without needing to purchase, install, or maintain the hardware locally.
  • Independent evaluation of the statistical quality, throughput, and reliability of the randomness produced by each device.
  • Entropy mixing across devices from different vendors, to help maximize the security of the combined random output.
  • A venue for vendors to have their QRNG devices evaluated and showcased alongside those of other manufacturers.

Status

The QRNG Zoo platform is currently being built. We are in the process of assembling the hardware and onboarding the first QRNG devices. Details on remote access, supported vendors, and evaluation tools will be posted here as they become available.
